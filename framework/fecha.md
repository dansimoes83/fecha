# F.E.C.H.A.
## Decision & Execution Framework

## Practical Playbook for Turning Work Into Decisions, Pilots, or Clear Next Steps

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Use case:** Product work, design work, creative projects, stakeholder alignment, internal communication, leadership routines, and personal execution systems.  
**Format:** Public, model-agnostic reference for humans, agents, assistants, collaborators, or personal operating systems.

---

## 1. What F.E.C.H.A. Is

F.E.C.H.A. is a practical decision and execution framework designed to prevent good work from staying open, vague, or unused.

It helps transform an idea, artifact, concept, prototype, proposal, message, meeting, or piece of work into one of three outcomes:

1. **A decision**
2. **A pilot or test**
3. **A clear next step with owner and timing**

The framework is especially useful when someone tends to create strong work, but leaves the next move dependent on open feedback, delayed validation, or other people noticing the value.

F.E.C.H.A. is not a full product methodology. It is a behavior and communication protocol.

Its role is simple:

> Do not let valuable work end with “What do you think?” when it should end with a recommendation, decision, pilot, or next step.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Every meaningful piece of work should leave the room with one of these outputs:

- **Decision made**
- **Pilot defined**
- **Next step assigned**

If none of these exists, the work is not closed yet.

A useful output is not only something that looks good, sounds clear, or has strong reasoning.

A useful output is something that can move forward.

---

## 3. When to Use F.E.C.H.A.

Use F.E.C.H.A. when preparing to:

- share a design direction;
- present a concept;
- send a proposal;
- ask for feedback;
- run or join a meeting;
- align stakeholders;
- create a prototype;
- write an update;
- document a process;
- request approval;
- move from exploration to execution;
- close a conversation;
- convert an idea into a test;
- turn work into something others can adopt.

F.E.C.H.A. is most useful when there is a risk of:

- vague feedback;
- silence after sharing;
- endless iteration;
- unclear ownership;
- no decision;
- no adoption;
- too much explanation and not enough movement;
- work being appreciated but not used.

---

## 4. The F.E.C.H.A. Sequence

F.E.C.H.A. stands for:

1. **Foco**
2. **Enquadramento**
3. **Caminho mínimo**
4. **Handoff de decisão**
5. **Amarração**

Each step answers a specific question.

| Step | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Foco | What needs to be decided or moved forward? | Clear objective |
| Enquadramento | Why does this matter? | Context and tension |
| Caminho mínimo | What is the smallest useful test or next move? | Pilot or practical path |
| Handoff de decisão | What response is needed from others? | Decision request |
| Amarração | What was decided and who does what next? | Closed loop |

---

## 5. Step 1 — Foco

### Purpose

Define what the work needs to resolve before producing, sharing, or discussing it.

This step prevents the work from becoming a broad conversation with no clear direction.

### Core question

> What needs to be decided, tested, or moved forward here?

### Avoid

- “I want to show this and see what people think.”
- “I want to share a first idea.”
- “I want to get general feedback.”
- “I want to explore possibilities.”

These can be valid starting points, but they are not enough for decision-making.

### Prefer

- “This needs to decide which direction we follow.”
- “This needs to validate if the pattern is good enough to reuse.”
- “This needs to confirm the first use case.”
- “This needs to identify what is blocking the next step.”

### Practical prompt

Before sharing anything, complete this sentence:

> The main decision or movement needed here is __________.

### Example

Weak:

> I’m sharing this concept to hear your thoughts.

Stronger:

> I’m sharing this concept to decide if this should become the base direction for the next applications.

---

## 6. Step 2 — Enquadramento

### Purpose

Explain the tension behind the work before showing the solution.

People evaluate better when they understand what problem, trade-off, or decision the work is addressing.

### Core question

> Why does this matter now?

### Avoid

- showing the solution without context;
- jumping straight into visual details;
- explaining process without explaining the decision;
- assuming people will understand the value by looking at the artifact.

### Prefer

- state the tension;
- explain the risk;
- name the trade-off;
- connect the work to a practical need.

### Practical prompt

Complete this sentence:

> The challenge is not only __________. The real tension is __________.

### Example

Weak:

> Here is the new design direction.

Stronger:

> The challenge is not only creating a stronger visual direction. The real tension is making sure this direction can work across product, communication, and commercial use without losing consistency.

---

## 7. Step 3 — Caminho mínimo

### Purpose

Define the smallest useful action that can put the work in contact with reality.

This step prevents overthinking, over-polishing, and waiting for perfect conditions.

### Core question

> What is the smallest useful test or next move?

### Avoid

- trying to solve the whole system at once;
- waiting until everything is polished;
- creating a complete package before validating the direction;
- asking for broad approval when a small test would teach more.

### Prefer

- one pilot;
- one use case;
- one prototype;
- one issue;
- one application;
- one small audience;
- one decision checkpoint.

### Practical prompt

Complete this sentence:

> The smallest useful next step is __________.

### Example

Weak:

> We can develop the full system and then review everything.

Stronger:

> We can test this direction in one use case first. If it works, we expand it.

---

## 8. Step 4 — Handoff de decisão

### Purpose

Make clear what kind of response is needed from other people.

This step turns feedback into decision-making.

### Core question

> What response do I need now?

### Avoid

- “What do you think?”
- “Any thoughts?”
- “Let me know your feedback.”
- “Feel free to comment.”

These are too open when a decision is needed.

### Prefer

- “My recommendation is...”
- “I need confirmation on...”
- “The decision needed now is...”
- “If this is aligned, the next step is...”
- “I suggest we move forward with...”

### Practical prompt

Complete this sentence:

> My recommendation is __________. What I need to validate now is __________.

### Example

Weak:

> What do you think about these options?

Stronger:

> I’m bringing two options. My recommendation is option 2 because it works better for the first use case. What I need to validate now is whether we can use it as the base direction.

---

## 9. Step 5 — Amarração

### Purpose

Close the loop after a conversation, meeting, review, or feedback exchange.

This step prevents silence, ambiguity, duplicated work, and loss of momentum.

### Core question

> What was decided, who owns the next step, and by when?

### Avoid

- ending with verbal agreement only;
- assuming everyone understood the same thing;
- leaving ownership implicit;
- moving to the next task without recording the decision.

### Prefer

- summarize the decision;
- name the next step;
- define the owner;
- define timing;
- document open points.

### Practical prompt

Use this format:

> To close the loop: we decided __________. The next step is __________. Owner: __________. Timing: __________.

### Example

Weak:

> Great, thanks everyone.

Stronger:

> To close the loop: we decided to move forward with option 2. The next step is to apply it to the first use case. I’ll prepare the update by Friday.

## 11. Quick Version for Agents

When assisting someone, check whether their message, plan, or artifact ends with a clear decision path.

If it ends with open feedback, remind them to apply F.E.C.H.A.

### Agent checklist

Ask:

1. What is the decision?
2. What is the context or tension?
3. What is the smallest useful next step?
4. What response is needed from others?
5. How will this be closed after the conversation?

If any answer is missing, suggest a sharper version.

### Agent reminder

> This is still too open. Apply F.E.C.H.A.: define the decision, explain the tension, propose a small next step, ask for specific validation, and close with owner and timing.

## 12. Communication Patterns

### Replace open feedback

Instead of:

> What do you think?

Use:

> My recommendation is to move forward with this direction. What I need to validate now is whether this is aligned enough for the next step.

---

### Replace vague sharing

Instead of:

> Sharing this for visibility.

Use:

> Sharing this to align on the direction before moving into the next step.

---

### Replace passive waiting

Instead of:

> Let me know your thoughts.

Use:

> If this direction is aligned, I’ll move forward with the next version by Friday.

---

### Replace broad approval

Instead of:

> Do you like this option?

Use:

> Does this option solve the use case well enough for us to test it in the first application?

---

### Replace meeting drift

Instead of:

> We discussed a few points today.

Use:

> To close the loop: we aligned on X, still need to decide Y, and the next step is Z.

## 13. Common Failure Modes

### Failure mode 1: Good work, no decision

Signal:

> The work is strong, but nobody knows what to do next.

Correction:

> Add Handoff de decisão and Amarração.

---

### Failure mode 2: Too much context, no recommendation

Signal:

> The explanation is detailed, but the point of view is unclear.

Correction:

> Add a clear recommendation.

---

### Failure mode 3: Feedback is too broad

Signal:

> People respond based on taste, preference, or scattered opinions.

Correction:

> Reframe the feedback around the decision needed.

---

### Failure mode 4: Work stays conceptual

Signal:

> The idea is well developed, but has no real-world application yet.

Correction:

> Define a Caminho mínimo.

---

### Failure mode 5: Verbal agreement disappears

Signal:

> Everyone seemed aligned, but nothing happens after the meeting.

Correction:

> Send an Amarração note after the conversation.

## 14. Practical Examples

### Example 1 — Design direction

Weak:

> I’m sharing this design direction to get your thoughts.

F.E.C.H.A. version:

> I’m sharing this design direction to decide if it can become the base for the next screens.  
> The main tension is consistency: we need something flexible enough for different states, but simple enough for implementation.  
> My suggestion is to test this direction in one flow first.  
> What I need to validate now is whether we agree with this direction before I move into the next version.  
> If aligned, I’ll apply it to the first flow by Friday.

---

### Example 2 — Creative project

Weak:

> Here are some character studies. What do you think?

F.E.C.H.A. version:

> I organized these character studies to decide which direction should become the base character.  
> The challenge is not only creating a strong character, but making sure it can work across product, communication, and commercial applications.  
> My recommendation is to move forward with option 2 and test it in one practical application first.  
> What I need to validate now is whether this direction feels aligned enough for a pilot.  
> If aligned, the next step is a small launch kit with one product mockup, one post, and one sticker.

---

### Example 3 — AI workflow or process

Weak:

> Sharing some learnings about the AI workflow.

F.E.C.H.A. version:

> I’m sharing this to validate whether the current AI-assisted workflow can become a repeatable base for future work.  
> The challenge is not only speed. The real tension is reducing ambiguity between requirements, design, and build.  
> My suggestion is to test the workflow in one complete work item before formalizing it.  
> What I need to validate now is whether this is useful enough to run as a pilot.  
> If aligned, I’ll document the pilot results and bring a recommendation next week.

---

### Example 4 — Meeting close

Weak:

> Thanks everyone. I’ll follow up.

F.E.C.H.A. version:

> To close the loop: we aligned on using this direction for the first use case.  
> The next step is to apply it to the prototype and review it with the team.  
> I’ll take the first version and share it by Thursday.  
> The only open point is the final copy.

## 15. Short Prompts for Daily Use

Use these prompts as quick reminders.

### Before starting work

> What decision will this work help make?

### Before sharing work

> What is my recommendation?

### Before asking feedback

> What kind of feedback do I actually need?

### Before expanding scope

> What is the smallest useful test?

### After a meeting

> What did we decide, who owns the next step, and by when?

### When stuck

> Is this still an idea, or is it ready to become a decision, pilot, or next step?

Do not end important work with only:

> “What do you think?”

> “My recommendation is this. The decision needed is this. If aligned, the next step is this.”
